# Terrain Reconstruction Plan

_Source basis: [PROJECT_BIBLE.md](PROJECT_BIBLE.md) and [RESORT_ANALYSIS.md](RESORT_ANALYSIS.md). This document is a reconstruction blueprint derived from the frame set and project requirements, not a survey drawing or engineering plan._

## Reconstruction Intent

The terrain must be rebuilt as a steep, terraced hillside resort carved into a mountain slope. The frames do not support a flat, generic site. They show a layered landform where circulation, architecture, and vegetation all respond to elevation change.

The reconstruction should prioritize these truths:

- The mountain edge is the primary spatial driver.
- The resort is stacked vertically, not spread horizontally.
- Paths climb repeatedly and reveal the site in stages.
- Retaining walls are structural and narrative elements.
- The pool is a high terrace destination, not a ground-level amenity.
- Vegetation is used to hide, frame, soften, and compress space.

## 1. Mountain Topology

The site should be reconstructed as a multi-band hillside that rises from the arrival edge toward the upper accommodation and pool zones.

### Topology structure

- **Lower approach band**: the entry road and first arrival court sit on the lowest visible operating level.
- **Mid-slope core band**: reception, parking, and the first major retaining structures occupy the middle of the slope.
- **Upper garden band**: cottages, bamboo corridors, and path landings step upward behind the public core.
- **Pool plateau band**: the infinity pool sits on a widened terrace above the main circulation spine.
- **Ridge / outlook band**: the topographic backdrop opens into a broad mountain silhouette and valley view.

### Shape language

- Use an asymmetric slope profile rather than a symmetric hill.
- Preserve a dominant downhill view axis toward the valley and distant ridge.
- Introduce shallow ridges and bowls in the surrounding terrain so the site feels embedded in a natural mountain flank.
- Keep the resort platforms artificially flattened, but let the surrounding terrain remain rolling and irregular.

## 2. Elevation Zones

The resort should be divided into clear elevation zones so the geometry supports the cinematic sequence.

### Recommended elevation bands

- **Zone A - Arrival base**: lowest drive-in and threshold edge.
- **Zone B - Reception terrace**: first major platform above the entry.
- **Zone C - Landscape transition**: stepped gardens, seating pockets, and short paths linking the public core to the residential zone.
- **Zone D - Private accommodation band**: cottages and villas tucked into the slope.
- **Zone E - Pool terrace**: highest completed amenity deck visible in the frames.
- **Zone F - Mountain background**: distant landform behind the resort, with haze and open sky.

### Relative level logic

- Each major resort band should be separated by a visible but walkable grade change.
- Platforms should feel cumulative, with repeated climbs of a few steps followed by a landing.
- The pool terrace should read as the highest moment in the guest route before the camera turns outward to the mountains.

## 3. Terrace Locations

The terrain reconstruction should include several distinct man-made terraces.

### Terrace 1 - Arrival / entrance apron

- Located at the first visible contact point with the resort.
- Supports the gate, boundary wall, and initial driveway turnaround.
- Must feel compressed and controlled.

### Terrace 2 - Reception / parking court

- The first large usable platform after the climb from the entry.
- Contains the reception building frontage and the parking cluster.
- Needs strong retaining edges because the frames show the court sitting above lower slopes.

### Terrace 3 - Garden transition court

- A smaller intermediate terrace linking reception to the private path network.
- Contains benches, lamp posts, planting pockets, and step-down / step-up connectors.
- Should appear as a landscaped buffer rather than a hard architectural platform.

### Terrace 4 - Cottage and villa ledges

- Multiple small terrace pads, each serving one or two units.
- These should be irregularly offset to avoid a repetitive housing grid.
- Must be narrow enough to preserve the sensation of hillside stacking.

### Terrace 5 - Pool deck

- The widest and most dramatic terrace in the resort.
- Should extend laterally enough to frame the pool, deck edge, and the long valley view.
- Needs a strong sense of enclosure from side walls and plant masses.

## 4. Retaining Wall Locations

Retaining walls are one of the clearest spatial signatures in the frames and should be modeled as a continuous structural system.

### Wall placement strategy

- **Wall run at the entry climb**: supports the driveway and gate approach.
- **Wall behind the reception court**: holds the upper garden terraces and separates public arrival from private circulation.
- **Wall under the stair network**: visible under and beside the stair flights connecting levels.
- **Wall around the parking court**: encloses the rear side of the arrival terrace.
- **Wall around the pool terrace**: forms the pool plateau edge and reinforces the hero reveal.
- **Short side walls beside path transitions**: control the narrow circulation passages.

### Wall language

- Use textured stone or stone-like masonry on the exposed faces.
- Combine high walls with occasional low parapets and stair retaining segments.
- Let walls step with the terrain rather than cutting as long uninterrupted slabs.
- Some walls should be partly softened with planting so they feel integrated into the slope.

## 5. Path Elevations

The path system should not be a single slope; it should rise in discrete cinematic increments.

### Path hierarchy

#### Main arrival path

- Starts low and climbs toward the reception terrace.
- Needs a broad enough grade to read as a vehicle arrival route.
- Should be visually clean and lightly flanked by walls and planting.

#### Mid-level pedestrian path

- Connects reception to the garden and accommodation zone.
- Uses shorter stair sequences and tighter turns.
- Should be partially hidden by shrubs, trees, and bamboo.

#### Upper garden path

- The most intimate path in the resort.
- Should run close to accommodation walls, benches, lamp posts, and small viewing pockets.
- Must feel like a discovery corridor leading toward the pool and lookout scenes.

#### Pool approach path

- Last approach before the pool reveal.
- Narrower, cleaner, and more deliberate than the lower paths.
- Should create a compressed reveal before the deck opens outward.

### Elevation behavior

- Paths should rise by steps and landings rather than continuous ramps wherever the frames show steepness.
- Each stair run should feel like a reveal break.
- Use shallow transitions near buildings and steeper transitions in planted sections.

## 6. Building Elevations

Buildings must sit into the slope, not on top of it.

### Entry / gate structures

- Positioned at the lower-to-mid transition zone.
- Their base should be anchored into a cut platform with visible boundary walls.
- The framing should show the gate slightly elevated over the approach.

### Reception building

- Set on a major mid-slope terrace.
- The floor plane should be level with the parking court and noticeably above the entry road.
- The lower facade should read as grounded into the terrace retaining edge.

### Cottage units

- Place cottages on smaller ledges above the reception band.
- Their bases should align to local slope cuts, with short stair access and planting breaks.
- The rooflines should remain prominent, but the foundations should visually disappear into the hillside.

### Villa units

- Villas should occupy slightly more exposed upper ledges and be more visible from the broad landscape shots.
- Their elevation should feel higher than the cottages and closer to the ridge-facing views.
- They should sit behind trees and partial screening so they are discovered gradually.

### Gazebo / swing pavilion

- Place this on an intermediate landscape terrace between public core and accommodation.
- It should occupy a small elevated garden pocket with a clear view of adjacent stairs and paths.

## 7. Infinity Pool Elevation

The pool is the highest emotional and visual plateau in the route and should be treated as a summit-level terrace.

### Pool terrace conditions

- The pool deck must be above the main residential circulation band.
- The terrace should feel isolated enough that the camera must intentionally arrive there.
- Side walls and planting should obscure the pool until the last approach.

### Pool relation to terrain

- The surrounding land should fall away toward the valley so the pool appears to hover over the landscape.
- The pool edge should align with the open view axis, allowing the mountain backdrop to read as part of the water composition.
- A secondary smaller water zone or adjoining basin may be kept as a supporting feature if visible in the reference interpretation.

## 8. Vegetation Placement

Vegetation must be reconstructed as a spatial system that controls visibility and reinforces the hillside geometry.

### Placement zones

- **Perimeter forest mass**: dense tree cover around the outer edges and upper slopes.
- **Bamboo corridors**: vertical screening along narrow stair transitions and side paths.
- **Entrance planting**: low flowering beds, shrubs, and small trees near the gate and arrival wall.
- **Reception garden pockets**: mixed ornamental planting around benches, lighting poles, and building fronts.
- **Accommodation screening**: dense shrubs and taller trees surrounding cottages and villas to create privacy.
- **Pool-edge softening**: planting clusters at the side and rear of the pool terrace to avoid a hard slab effect.
- **Valley-edge canopy**: open taller trees framing the view beyond the resort.

### Vegetation behavior by elevation

- Lower zones should use layered planting to disguise cuts and retaining edges.
- Mid zones should be the densest, because the frames show the paths being enclosed.
- Upper zones should open slightly to support the mountain-view reveal.
- Bamboo should be reserved for the most intimate transition corridors and stair landings.

## 9. Reconstruction Sequence

If rebuilding the terrain from scratch, the order should be:

1. Establish the mountain silhouette and slope direction.
2. Block out the lowest arrival band and upper ridgeline.
3. Cut the reception terrace and parking platform.
4. Step the garden and accommodation terraces upward.
5. Carve the pool plateau as the highest usable deck.
6. Insert retaining walls to stabilize every major break.
7. Lay the path network from low to high in the same order shown in the frames.
8. Place vegetation last to control concealment and reveal.

## 10. Blueprint Summary

The terrain should read as a sculpted Western Ghats hillside with a formal resort insertion layered into it. The most important reconstruction principle is not symmetry or efficiency, but staged revelation.

The site must be built so that:

- Arrival feels low and compressed.
- Reception feels elevated but still partial.
- Paths climb and narrow through vegetation.
- Accommodation appears tucked into the slope.
- The pool arrives as a summit-like reveal.
- The final views re-open the whole resort into the mountain landscape.

This terrain is the foundation of the cinematic experience and should be treated as the structural map for all later environment modeling.
